THE FORMULA

Understand the relationship

Ohm's law describes the relationship between voltage, current, and resistance in an electrical circuit.

VoltageV = I × R
CurrentI = V ÷ R
ResistanceR = V ÷ I
Remember

Use consistent units: volts (V), amperes (A), and ohms (Ω).

POWER EQUATIONS

Electrical power

Power describes the rate at which electrical energy is transferred or used.

PowerP = V × I
Using currentP = I² × R
Using voltageP = V² ÷ R
Units

Power is measured in watts (W). These basic equations assume compatible values for the same DC circuit operating point.

SERIES CIRCUIT

One path for current

In a series circuit, resistors are connected end-to-end. Equivalent resistance is the sum of their resistances.

R₁R₂R₃
Example

100 Ω + 220 Ω + 330 Ω = 650 Ω total resistance.

PARALLEL CIRCUIT

Multiple current paths

In a parallel circuit, each resistor shares the same voltage. Equivalent resistance is lower than the smallest positive branch resistance.

R₁R₂R₃
Formula

Rₜ = 1 ÷ (1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + … + 1/Rₙ)
